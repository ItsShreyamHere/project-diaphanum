Project Diaphanum

An open archive of Indian public procurement, 2011–2026

Government tenders let us citizens know where our tax rupees get spent. Every tender in India must be published on an official e-procurement portal. The records are scattered over about forty portals, making scrutiny extremely inaccessible to the average citizen.

Diaphanum collects them into one place. This release holds 18.9 million tender notices and 8.8 million awards of contract from every Indian state and union territory, in open formats, open-sourced to make the rightful critique of the system of governance more accessible.

It builds on the archive of Sarthak Siddhant, which covers the central government well but is thin on the states. Diaphanum adds the states. When talking about open-sourcing public procurement data, it is impossible to do so without acknowledging the work of Sarthak Siddhant. My sincerest gratitudes to him for kickstarting a movement where citizens gather on digital platforms to collaborate actively on scrutiny of public data. Kudos.

This project initially began in June 2025, with the aim of gathering public procurement data from my home state, West Bengal. Since then, it has exceeded multiple versions of its scope, and I believe this is the right time to release the data gathered until now. In no way is Project Diaphanum complete: there are several more organisations to examine, and closer inspection is required in the tallies of some states, but what good is this data if it sits on a hard disk and is not used to gain insights into the proceedings that build and run our country.

  • Notices (tenders, tender_details, tender_details_extra): what was put out to tender, by whom, when, for how much, where, in which category.
  • Awards (aoc_tenders, aoc_details): published awards of contract, with organisation, value, date and winner.
  • Bidders (award_bidders): every bidder on those awards, with status and bid value where the portal shows it.
  • Bid lists without an award (bid_lists_no_published_award): tenders where the portal shows who bid but never published an award.
  • Inferred awards (inferred_awards, 2,928,577 rows): for those tenders, the likely winner, chosen by L1 rank (1,083,948), sole financial bidder (1,745,259) or lowest price (99,370). Kept in a separate table and never mixed with published awards. Each row is marked inferred from bid list; award not published. The lowest bidder does not always get the contract.

Sources

Every record was read from an official government e-procurement portal, and every added row carries the URL of the page it came from, so any record can be checked against its source. Only publicly accessible pages were used. Nothing is login-only, paid or leaked.

NIC GePNIC portals

The e-procurement system of the National Informatics Centre, used by about 30 states and union territories: West Bengal, Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Madhya Pradesh, Haryana, Odisha, Punjab, Rajasthan, Delhi, Jammu & Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, Jharkhand, Uttarakhand, Assam, Tripura, Goa, Chandigarh, Puducherry, Ladakh, Andaman & Nicobar, Dadra & Nagar Haveli, Daman & Diu, Lakshadweep and the north-eastern states. For example wbtenders.gov.in, mahatenders.gov.in, etender.up.nic.in, etenders.kerala.gov.in, tntenders.gov.in.

How it was checked

  • No duplicates. Each tender appears once per table, and no added row repeats a tender already in the earlier archive (checked by tender id).
  • No id clashes. Some portals use bare numbers as ids, which collide with other portals. Their ids carry a prefix: TS_ Telangana, AP_ Andhra Pradesh, CG_ Chhattisgarh, GJNP_ Gujarat.
  • Against the official count. Notice totals match the NIC eProcurement dashboard (eprocure.gov.in/eprocdashboard) at about 100% for every GePNIC state, except Manipur (7%) and Ladakh (97%).
  • Dates. Award dates that did not parse as a real date were left blank, not guessed.
  • Files. Row counts of every table were verified against the manifests, and both SQLite files pass PRAGMA quick_check.
  • Source untouched. The earlier archive was only read, never modified.

Limitations of this release

The release cannot be deemed complete. There are clearly many gaps, as seen in the State-wise distribution table, with some states having much less tenders than expected. A closer look into these topics is necessary.

  • Several states publish financial-evaluation results without a formal award notice. In Kerala, Tripura, West Bengal, Maharashtra, Goa, Assam, Tamil Nadu, Odisha and Punjab, the archive holds 53–84% of the count on the NIC dashboard; the rest is not published per tender. Tamil Nadu does not show bidders before an award.
  • Chhattisgarh, Gujarat and Bihar have few awards, because those portals publish few.
  • Values and dates are as each portal shows them. Nothing is converted to a single format or currency.
